GENERATION STORAGE (What you asked about in detail):

generation-apks uses a HYBRID THREE-LAYER model:

  Layer 1: Package Store (read-only, content-addressed)
  ├── /var/lib/kodos-alpine/store/bash/5.3.9-r1/files/
  ├── /var/lib/kodos-alpine/store/openssh/8.9p1-r2/files/
  └── /var/lib/kodos-alpine/store/zsh/5.9-r3/files/
      
  Layer 2: Generation Skeleton (symlinks + mutable /etc)
  ├── /var/lib/kodos-alpine/generation/0/
  │   ├── bin -> /store/bash/5.3.9-r1/bin        (symlink)
  │   ├── usr -> /store/bash/5.3.9-r1/usr        (symlink)
  │   ├── etc/                                    (real, editable)
  │   ├── etc_default/                            (pristine defaults)
  │   └── etc_store/                              (working copy)
  └── /var/lib/kodos-alpine/generation/1/
      └── ... (reuses store entries, adds new packages)

  Layer 3: Atomic Pointer
  └── /var/lib/kodos-alpine/generation/current -> generation/1
      (ONE symlink change = instant rollback)

WHY THIS MATTERS:
  • Storage efficiency: 73% less than full-chroot model (540 MB vs 2 GB for 20 gens)
  • Config tracking: /etc_default vs /etc_store detects user edits
  • Atomic activation: Symlink repoint is instant, cannot be half-way
  • Rollback flexibility: Can reboot to old generation or rebuild from old config

STORAGE COMPARISON: Three Approaches

Approach A (Full Chroot) — What we do in MVP:
  Gen 0: 100 MB
  Gen 1: 100 MB
  Gen 2: 100 MB
  ────────────
  20 gens: 2000 MB
  
  Pros: Simple, clear, obvious what's where
  Cons: Storage overhead
  Good for: MVP (fast implementation)

Approach B (Store-Based) — What we do in v1.0:
  Store: 500 MB (all packages, once)
  Gen 0: 5 MB (symlinks + /etc)
  Gen 1: 5 MB (symlinks + /etc)
  Gen 2: 5 MB (symlinks + /etc)
  ────────────
  20 gens: 540 MB
  
  Savings: 73% vs. full chroot
  Pros: Storage efficient, elegant, tracks changes
  Cons: Symlink complexity (mitigated by generation-apks reference)
  Good for: Production (migrate after MVP)

Approach C (Btrfs Snapshots) — Future consideration:
  Btrfs CoW: 520 MB (filesystem handles snapshots)
  
  Pros: Best efficiency, seamless rollback
  Cons: Requires Btrfs, less portable, more complex
  Good for: v2+ (after MVP and v1.0 work)

RECOMMENDATION: Start with A, migrate to B, consider C later.
